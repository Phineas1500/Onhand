A Sidenote alternative that marks up the page as it answers.

Onhand and Sidenote are both AI reading assistants that show where an answer comes from. In Sidenote, you click a citation to jump to the sentence it came from. Onhand highlights the passages as it answers, leaves a note in the margin beside each one, and can tutor you with Learning mode. It's free to start, needs no account, and is open source.

Built for the page you're reading

  • Evidence without clicking: the passages behind an answer light up on the page while Onhand answers, so you can check each point where it's written.
  • Notes in the margin: a sentence or two beside each highlight, so you keep your place on the page.
  • Citations followed: ask what a reference like [14] says, and Onhand opens the cited paper and answers from it.
  • A tutor when you want one: Learning mode asks before it tells and quizzes you on the page.

When Sidenote fits better

Sidenote reaches more places. Its connectors read Google Docs, Notion and Confluence, and it works with Gmail and Outlook, YouTube videos with timestamped citations, Word and PowerPoint uploads, and scanned PDFs with OCR on paid plans. It also keeps a searchable library you can ask across, runs in Firefox and as a desktop app, and offers team plans. If you work mostly in those tools, Sidenote covers them; Onhand is focused on the webpages and PDFs you read in Chrome.

Try it

Add Onhand to Chrome, open the page you're reading, and click the Onhand icon or press Ctrl+Shift+Space (⌘+Shift+Space on a Mac). Onhand Free needs no account or API key and has a daily limit; you can also sign in with your ChatGPT plan or use your own OpenAI, Anthropic, Google Gemini or OpenRouter key.
