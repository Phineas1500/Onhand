A NotebookLM alternative that works on the page you're reading.

Onhand is a free, open-source AI Chrome extension that answers questions about the webpage or PDF you have open and highlights the passages it used right there. Google's NotebookLM, renamed Gemini Notebook in July 2026, works on sources you add to a notebook in its own app.

Ask while you read, without building a notebook

  • No setup: open a reading, a paper or a course page and ask. There is no notebook to create and nothing to add first.
  • The evidence stays in place: Onhand highlights the passages it used on the page you're reading, so you see each one in its context instead of in a separate citation view.
  • Pages behind a sign-in work: course sites and library journal access, because Onhand reads the page in your browser.
  • Pick up later: sessions are saved in your browser with their highlights, notes and answers.

When NotebookLM fits better

If you want to gather a whole course's sources into one place and ask across all of them, or you want audio and video overviews, mind maps or slide decks made from your sources, NotebookLM is built for that. It also has mobile apps; Onhand is a Chrome extension.

Using both

They work well together: use NotebookLM for an overview of a stack of sources, and Onhand when you sit down to read one of them closely.

Try it

Add Onhand to Chrome, open the page you're reading, and click the Onhand icon or press Ctrl+Shift+Space (⌘+Shift+Space on a Mac). Onhand Free needs no account or API key and has a daily limit; you can also sign in with your ChatGPT plan or use your own OpenAI, Anthropic, Google Gemini or OpenRouter key.
