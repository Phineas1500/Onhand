# AI Chrome extensions for reading PDFs, compared.

Eight AI tools that answer questions about a PDF while you read it in Chrome, and what each one does best. Onhand makes one of them, so this page also says when each of the others is the better pick.

[Add Onhand to Chrome](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/ogjmncmkpgdkkcibdiacmagaehjohljb)

Onhand is free to start, needs no account and is open source. Also on [Firefox](https://addons.mozilla.org/firefox/addon/onhand-ai/) and [Edge](https://microsoftedge.microsoft.com/addons/detail/icbhigkjjhkcheejmdfkfhiljnkommoj).

## Pick by what you need

-   **See the answer highlighted where it is in the PDF, with no account:** Onhand.
-   **Nothing to install, on a computer:** Gemini in Chrome, which is built into the browser.
-   **Edit, convert and sign PDFs as well:** Adobe Acrobat, with its AI Assistant.
-   **One sidebar with many AI models for everything you do online:** Sider.
-   **Research papers, and finding related papers:** SciSpace.
-   **Reading across Gmail, Google Docs, Notion and Slack too:** Sidenote.
-   **Highlighting and keeping notes as you read:** Glasp.
-   **A library of PDFs you chat with, on paid plans across all of them:** PDF.ai.

## What to check before you pick

-   **Where you read:** some tools work on the PDF you already have open in Chrome; others open it in their own viewer or ask you to upload it to their library first.
-   **Whether you can check the answer:** a page number tells you where to look; a highlight on the passage itself shows you the evidence in context.
-   **What the free use covers:** free plans are counted per day, per month or per document, and some cover only part of the features.
-   **Whether you need an account:** most of these tools need one, even on a free plan.

## Where Onhand fits

Onhand is built for close reading: you ask about the PDF or webpage you have open, and it highlights the passages it used right there, so you can check each one in context. Learning mode adds guiding questions and quizzes tied to the page. It's a browser extension, so there's no phone app, and it doesn't edit or sign PDFs or keep a library of uploaded files; if you need those, one of the other tools fits better.

## Try it

Add Onhand to Chrome, open a PDF, and click the Onhand icon or press `Ctrl+Shift+Space` (`⌘+Shift+Space` on a Mac). Onhand Free needs no account or API key and has a daily limit; you can also sign in with your ChatGPT plan or use your own OpenAI, Anthropic, Google Gemini or OpenRouter key.
