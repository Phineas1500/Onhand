How Honeybees Share Directions

When a forager finds a rich patch of flowers, she returns to the hive and performs a waggle dance on the vertical face of the comb.

The angle of the waggle run relative to vertical tells her sisters the direction of the food relative to the sun. A run straight up the comb means "fly toward the sun."

The duration of the waggle run encodes distance: longer runs mean the flowers are farther away, roughly one second of waggling for every kilometre.

For food very close to the hive, foragers perform a round dance instead, which signals "search nearby" without giving a precise direction.
